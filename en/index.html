How it works

One module, one cable per brand

The module is the same for every bike: only the cable changes from one brand to the next. KT first; Bafang, Lishui, Rad, Lectric and Aventon next, each once it's verified on a real controller. Your phone connects to it wirelessly and becomes the display.

  1. Plug in the module

    Unplug the original display and plug Kurant in its place, with its KT cable. No soldering, and the original display plugs back in any time.

  2. Open the app

    The app, on iPhone or Android, finds the module and recognizes the controller type.

  3. Ride

    Turn the bike on from the app. Data shows live and you change assist on the screen. Settings are changed when stopped.

Safety

Once on, the bike never depends on the phone

  • No phone

    The bike keeps running

    The module keeps the link with the controller on its own. A dead phone, a closed app or an update in progress does not cut the assist.

  • > 0.5 km/h

    No settings while riding

    Above 0.5 km/h, or when the speed is unknown, sensitive settings are locked.

  • 1.5 s

    No frozen values

    With no new data for 1.5 seconds, the screen shows “--” instead of an old number.

  • Verified

    Settings open only after verification

    A setting can be changed only once its encoding has been measured on a real controller. The most important ones need a typed confirmation.

  • 32 km/h

    Street mode by default

    Out of the box, speed is limited to your province's legal maximum: 32 km/h in Quebec.

  • Offroad

    A mode for private land

    It switches on only when stopped, with a 1.5-second press followed by a confirmation. On public roads, a bike set above 32 km/h no longer counts as a power-assisted bicycle. The rider is responsible for how this mode is used.

Frequently asked questions

Does it work with iPhone?

Yes. The app will be free on iPhone and Android, with no account.

What happens if my phone dies?

Mid-ride, the bike keeps the assist level you chose; you lose the display until the phone reconnects. If your phone is dead before a ride, plug the original display back in: it takes a few seconds, no tools.

How do I turn the bike on?

From the app. The module then keeps the bike on, even if the phone disconnects.

Is a phone mount included?

No: use your own handlebar mount. One with charging is a good idea for long rides.

Does installation need soldering?

No. Kurant plugs into the original display's connector, with a cable made for your controller. Keep the original display: you can plug it back in at any time.

Where does the module go?

On the front handlebar, where the original display was, on the same connector.

Which bikes will be supported?

At launch, KT controllers (LCD3, LCD5, LCD8 displays) on a 48 or 52 V battery: they are bench-verified. Bafang UART, Lishui, Rad Power Bikes, Lectric and Aventon (48 V) are in development; 36 V systems such as Shimano STEPS come later. Reserve your system for $1 on Kickstarter: the most reserved go on the bench first. Bosch isn't planned: its system is closed. Use the checker above for your bike.

How much will it cost?

The planned price is CAD 159 plus tax, module and app included. During the Kickstarter campaign: CAD 119 for the first 100, then CAD 139. The app has no subscription.

Do I need an account?

No. The app doesn't ask for an account, and your ride history stays on your phone.

When will Kurant be available?

The prototype already works on a real KT controller; what's left is adding the other brands' profiles. Kurant launches through a Kickstarter campaign, delivered in Canada about six months after it ends: final board and enclosure, certification, then production. The waitlist hears first.

Why “Kurant”?

From the French courant: the electric current that moves your bike, and being au courant, in the know about everything it does.
